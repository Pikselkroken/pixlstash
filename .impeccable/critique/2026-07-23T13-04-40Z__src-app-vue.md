## Design Specificity Verdict

**Authored for this product where it matters; generic in the ambient chrome.**

**LLM assessment:** The interaction model is unmistakably built for AI-image triage at volume — Smart-Score / Character-Likeness / Likeness-Groups sorts with a strictness slider (only meaningful when you're drowning in near-duplicate generations), the "Scrapheap" cull-and-auto-purge loop, keyboard-first digit scoring, and above all the **Tag Review assembly line** ("not tagged 'malformed hand', but 6 of 12 similar images have it · tagger 54% sure" → Y/N/Skip/Undo). None of that could be lifted from a stock DAM. It goes category-interchangeable only in the chrome: an undifferentiated MDI icon row, and the selection wash that actively fights the "Quiet Darkroom" identity.

**Deterministic scan:** detect.mjs returned 29 raw hits (exit 2), but 9 are false positives and most of the rest are design-justified once read against the brief:
- **broken-image ×9 — FALSE POSITIVES.** Every hit matched the literal string `<img>` inside JS/JSDoc comments, not real empty-src tags (Vue binds via `:src`). Real count is 20, not 29.
- **side-tab ×8 (3px colored left border) — mostly NOT defects.** These are PixlStash's *prescribed* language: the `--active-bar` selection edge (visual-language.md §11) in `SideBar.vue`/`FolderTreeNode.vue`, and the intentional add/remove banner accent on `ReviewBinaryCard`/`ReviewPairCard`. The detector's generic "AI tell" rule collides with a committed design decision — **the brief wins.**
- **bounce-easing ×3 — design-justified.** `--ease-spring` + the celebration/sticker "pop" are the deliberate "Pretend this is fun" gamification. (Tension worth noting: DESIGN.md's motion section says no bounce; the shipped app added a named spring token for the gamification opt-in. That's a conscious exception, not drift — but the docs and code should agree.)
- **layout-transition ×9 (`transition: width`) — mixed.** Progress/meter fills (`ProgressOverlay`, `StatsSidebar`, review rails) animate width by design; a couple (`App.css`, `ImageOverlay`) are genuine perf nits worth converting to transform.

Net: the deterministic scan surfaced **almost no genuine design defect** once the brief is applied — the real problems came from the human-lens review below. That's the headline: the code is *on-system*; the issues are *design decisions*, chiefly the selection state.

**Visual overlays:** none — live browser injection was not available (the app needs a running backend + authenticated login). Judgment used the real product screenshots as static evidence.

## Overall Impression

This is a genuinely well-built Operate tool with a real point of view: it knows its user is culling thousands of AI generations and it optimizes ruthlessly for that. The keyboard model and the Tag Review flow are standout, product-specific craft. The single biggest opportunity is small and everywhere: the most frequent state in the entire app — a selected tile — is painted a cold hospital-blue that breaks the product's own warm "darkroom" identity. Fix that one thing and the app's most-seen moment finally looks like the brand.

## What's Working

1. **Real keyboard-driven bulk operation** (`useGridKeyboardNav.js`): arrow/shift-range/Ctrl+A/space-toggle, digit scoring on hover or selection, T-to-tag, viewport-extend — and it correctly bails when focus is in an input. For "score 500 frames," this is the difference between usable and not.
2. **The Tag Review assembly line**: one image, one question, full context (similar-images rail + tagger confidence), reversible, progress always visible — the clearest embodiment of "AI sorts, human judges."
3. **The Scrapheap model**: reversible-by-default delete with auto-purge countdown and explicit protection for reference originals — a mature, product-specific safety net.

## Priority Issues

**[P0] Selection wash is cold info-blue on the signature component**
- **Why it matters:** Selection is the most frequent state in an Operate workflow and this is *the* signature component. `.selection-overlay` uses `rgba(var(--v-theme-info),.38)` — cold blue over warm photos — plus a hardcoded radius, while the keyboard cursor uses olive `primary`. It breaks the Warmth Rule and the One-Safelight Rule, and makes "selected" and "cursor here" read as two unrelated systems. Detector didn't catch this (it's a color *choice*, not a pattern) — pure design-review finding.
- **Fix:** Replace the blue fill with the prescribed amber `--active-wash` + `--active-bar` edge, tuned per theme; unify the keyboard cursor onto the same accent focus-ring; tokenize the radius (`--radius-md`).
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable colorize` (then `/impeccable harden` to hold it on-token).

**[P1] Grid is invisible to assistive tech — violates a stated product requirement**
- **Why it matters:** PRODUCT.md makes full keyboard operability a requirement. Tiles are `<div>`s with no `role`/`tabindex`/`aria-selected`; `.thumbnail-img` has **no `alt`**; there's no `aria-live` for selection count or score changes; nav is a window-level `keydown` listener with no roving focus. Excellent for sighted power users, invisible to Sam.
- **Fix:** `role="grid"`/`gridcell` with roving `tabindex` + `aria-selected`; meaningful `alt`; a visually-hidden `aria-live="polite"` announcing selection count and scoring; accent focus-ring as the visible indicator.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable harden`.

**[P1] Icon-only chrome forces recall and hides the product's best feature**
- **Why it matters:** Search/Export/Import/ComfyUI/Review/Settings/Stats are icon-only (tooltip-only), and the rich shortcut vocabulary is surfaced only via F1 — so new users never discover that number keys score, which is the whole point of the tool. Fails Recognition-over-Recall; taxes working memory every session.
- **Fix:** Persistent text labels or inline shortcut hints on the highest-frequency actions (`Search F`, `Tag T`); teach "1–5 to score" on first hover; make the right cluster hierarchical (one primary, rest quieter). Keep F1 as the full reference.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable clarify` (+ a light `/impeccable onboard` for shortcut discovery).

**[P2] Context menu is a 12-item wall**
- **Why it matters:** The right-click menu lists 12 actions with one divider — far past the ≤4-per-decision guideline, on a surface used constantly during triage.
- **Fix:** Group into ~4 labeled clusters (Organize · AI · Search · Danger) or fold the low-frequency AI ops (Tag automatically / Generate description / Segment) under an "AI actions" submenu.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable distill`.

**[P2] Off-token drift: empty state + a few genuine layout animations**
- **Why it matters:** `.empty-state-title` isn't Tiny5 and both lines are sized in `em` (the exact drift DESIGN.md names as its top typographic risk) — and empty states are a sanctioned brand moment. Separately, `App.css` / `ImageOverlay` animate `width` where `transform` would be smoother. (The side-tab and bounce-easing detector hits are design-justified — see the specificity verdict — and should be *left alone* or reconciled in the docs, not "fixed.")
- **Fix:** Tiny5 `--text-2xl` headline + `--text-sm` guidance in rem; convert the genuine `width` transitions to `transform`; align DESIGN.md's motion section with the intentional `--ease-spring` gamification token.
- **Suggested command:** `/impeccable typeset` (+ `/impeccable polish`).

## Persona Red Flags

**Alex (power user)** — the keyboard model is built for him; happy overall. Real friction: DEL sends a large selection to the Scrapheap with **no in-place Undo toast** — a mis-key on 200 images is only recoverable by navigating to the heap. The selection wash annoys every session.

**Sam (accessibility / screen-reader / keyboard)** — **fails the product's own requirement.** Unlabeled sea of `div`s (no roles, no `alt`), no roving focus, no `aria-live` — can't tell what's focused, selected, or that a score landed. The floating "(N) selected" and the pulsing task light are visual-only.

**Devin (AI-image generator, dark-mode, thousands of ComfyUI outputs)** — the product is *for* him and largely delivers (likeness-groups strictness, smart-score sort, hover-scoring, bulk ComfyUI runs, malformed-hand Tag Review). Red flags: he lives in the **cold-blue selection wash** all day (P0 hits him hardest); a fat-fingered bulk cull has **no immediate undo**; and his single most valuable shortcut (number-key scoring) is hidden behind F1.

- Status badges correctly pair icon + text (purge countdown, format) — Status-Is-Not-Color-Alone honored. The *ambient* status system (filter/task badges, activity light) is more on-brand than the *selection* system.
- Lightbox rejected-tags row shows confidence (`nose 49%`) — honest, but the check/× targets are tiny.

## Questions to Consider

1. If selection is the most-seen state in the app, why is it the one place you spent a cold, non-accent color? What would the darkroom feel like if selecting a frame made it *glow amber* instead of *go blue*?
2. The keyboard model is the product's superpower and it's hidden behind F1 — what if the first-run grid taught "press 1–5 to score" on first hover, the way a game teaches its controls?
3. You built a beautiful assembly line for reviewing *tags* — why isn't *culling* a first-class assembly line too (one big image, keep/scrap/skip, running tally, instant undo) instead of grid-plus-DEL?
4. The context menu carries 12 verbs because everything is reachable from everywhere. What are the 4 verbs a triager actually right-clicks for?
5. Accessibility is a written requirement, yet the grid is invisible to screen readers. Is "keyboard-driven" being conflated with "accessible" — and who signs off that the requirement is met?
